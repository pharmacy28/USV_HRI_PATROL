\title{Predictive Human Search Responsibility for\\Collaborative Multi-USV Probabilistic Search}
\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{abstract}
An operator's future search activity can affect how an autonomous fleet should allocate effort. This paper formulates predictive human search responsibility for multi-USV probabilistic search. Sensor evidence updates a normalized target belief, while an explicit operator SEARCH declaration activates a separate planning responsibility. A reactive condition anchors that responsibility at the current human-controlled vehicle position. A predictive condition uses a valid future-position estimate and falls back to the current position when prediction is unavailable. Navigation, cancellation, and control changes revoke obsolete responsibility. Autonomous centers are optimized with fixed human anchors before obstacle-aware path-cost assignment. A planned paired evaluation tests predictive information under a shared planning pipeline. Its primary outcome is restricted mean time to sustained target proximity, assessed independently using simulator truth. This effective encounter is not visual detection or confirmation. Comparative results remain pending.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
human--robot collaboration, multi-robot search, unmanned surface vehicles, responsibility allocation, motion prediction
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Probabilistic robotic search couples uncertain target-location estimates with repeated spatial allocation~\cite{b1,b7,b8}. In human--multi-USV search, an operator may take control, navigate, search, or interrupt a task. Autonomy must account for these interventions without treating human motion as evidence that the target is present.

The question is deliberately narrow: \emph{does predicting where an explicitly searching operator will be improve fleet coordination beyond using the operator's current position?} Human search prediction and anticipatory coordination are established topics~\cite{b2,b3,b4}. Our hypothesis concerns the decision value of a future-position anchor in belief-weighted fleet allocation, together with withdrawal when the underlying search commitment ends.

Takeover alone does not establish search responsibility. The operator declares SEARCH; prediction then estimates where that declared activity may occur. NAVIGATE may still generate observations and a target encounter, but does not reserve a search region. This separates execution authority, task commitment, target inference, and fleet allocation.

The intended contributions are: (i) an explicit search-responsibility contract separated from target evidence; (ii) predictive fixed-center redistribution with revocation and fallback semantics; and (iii) an evaluation isolating predictive information from shared planning and execution. Bayesian filtering, Random Forest (RF), weighted clustering, A*, and Hungarian assignment are shared components rather than individual novelty claims. Whether prediction improves search remains an experimental hypothesis.

\section{Related Work and Scope}
Bourgault \emph{et al.} studied coordinated Bayesian search~\cite{b1}; multi-robot search and exploration also require explicit sensing uncertainty~\cite{b7,b8}. We use probabilistic inference as a common task state, not a new filter.

Xie \emph{et al.} studied intention-based cooperative search~\cite{b2}, while Heintzman \emph{et al.} incorporated anticipated human trajectories in search-and-rescue planning~\cite{b3}. Intent and action prediction are broader HRI foundations~\cite{b4}. Therefore, first prediction of human search behavior is not claimed. The narrower question is whether future-position information changes responsibility allocation enough to improve over current-position awareness.

Dalmasso \emph{et al.} investigated shared task representation in collaborative navigation and search~\cite{b5}. For our system, goal authority, execution authority, and permission to replace a plan are distinct analytical concepts; these are not attributed verbatim to that work. A navigation goal can be human selected yet autonomously executed, without establishing a search commitment. 

\section{Problem and Search Contract}
\subsection{Environment and Inference}
A known planar obstacle map defines searchable free cells $\mathcal X=\{x_i\}_{i=1}^N$. Exactly one stationary target occupies an unknown free cell. A fleet of $n$ USVs has positions $p_r(t)$; the VRX platform is configured for ten WAM-Vs. Simulator truth is unavailable to planning and target inference.

Legal sensor observations $Z_{1:k}$ produce the normalized belief
\begin{equation}
 b_i^k=P(X=x_i\mid Z_{1:k}),\qquad \sum_i b_i^k=1.
\end{equation}
For observation $z_k$ and sensor geometry $s_k$, the shared update is
\begin{equation}
 b_i^{k+1}=\frac{b_i^k L(z_k\mid X=x_i,s_k)}{\sum_j b_j^k L(z_k\mid X=x_j,s_k)}.
\label{eq:bayes}
\end{equation}
For a qualified binary opportunity with negative outcome, an assumed likelihood is $L_i(0)=1-P_{D,i}$. The opportunity definition, likelihood, and numerical parameters must be frozen with the implementation. This assumption does not demonstrate calibrated camera performance. Positive events need their own justified likelihood and are not encounter-success events. No completed calibration or continuous-time hazard model is assumed here.

Commands, gaze, joystick inputs, declarations, and predictions do not directly enter~\eqref{eq:bayes}. Legitimate sensing continues in SEARCH and NAVIGATE. Geometric visitation alone does not clear a cell.

\subsection{Explicit Human Search Responsibility}
The operator directly controls at most one vehicle $h(t)$. Directly controlled and active command-locked vehicles are excluded from autonomous reassignment. An execution goal and a search anchor are separate: retaining a NAVIGATE command does not require using its destination as a search center.

Let $a_h(t)$ indicate active explicit SEARCH. Takeover initializes $a_h=0$. A discrete declaration activates it, and the state persists between actions. NAVIGATE, cancellation, release, control-segment termination, or switching vehicles revokes it. A new vehicle requires a new declaration. The declaration answers whether the operator searches, not where the vehicle will be; no advance destination is required.

\section{Predictive Responsibility Redistribution}
\subsection{Current and Future Anchors}
During active SEARCH, the B2 and B3 anchors are
\begin{align}
 c_h^{B2}(t)&=p_h(t),\\
 c_h^{B3}(t)&=\begin{cases}\hat p_h(t+H),&v_h(t)=1,\\p_h(t),&v_h(t)=0,\end{cases}
\label{eq:anchor}
\end{align}
where $H$ is prediction horizon and $v_h$ denotes validity. Without active SEARCH, neither condition inserts a human search anchor. Missing prediction must not block fleet planning. Horizon, validity criteria, refresh rate, and prediction-driven replanning thresholds remain to be determined independently of final mission outcomes.

The predictor estimates future vehicle position from causal state/control history, not hidden intention or target location. Current-position and constant-velocity references precede a learned candidate such as RF. Training/validation splits separate runs and, where applicable, participants. Samples crossing task/control interruptions must be identified rather than labeled as uninterrupted SEARCH. Gaze and voice remain optional interface assets, not additional core contributions or prerequisites for this comparison.

\subsection{Fixed-Center Allocation and Execution}
With fixed anchors $\mathcal C_F$ and autonomous centers $\mathcal C_A$, the shared surrogate is
\begin{equation}
 \Phi=\sum_{i=1}^N b_i\min_{c\in\mathcal C_A\cup\mathcal C_F}\|x_i-c\|_2^2.
\label{eq:cluster}
\end{equation}
Fixed anchors remain fixed while autonomous centers update. All searchable free cells remain in the responsibility domain with deterministic tie-breaking. Zero-weight cells contribute zero to~\eqref{eq:cluster} but still receive geometric responsibility labels. No residual region is manually deleted and no artificial probability mass is introduced to repair allocation behavior. Planned responsibility does not guarantee actual sensing or create navigation exclusions.

For each eligible autonomous vehicle--center pair, A* first computes an obstacle-aware path $\mathcal P_{rj}$. Path-dependent costs then define assignment, for example
\begin{equation}
 \min_\pi\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_A} L(\mathcal P_{r,\pi(r)}).
\end{equation}
Hungarian assignment follows cost construction. Path length is a candidate baseline to freeze in the experiment manifest; estimated travel time is a separate alternative. Both conditions share region coverage and tracking. A* provides geometric paths, not a guarantee of WAM-V dynamic feasibility.

\subsection{Interruption and Fallback}
Predictions and declarations must be associated with the current mission, vehicle, and control/task segment. Ending a segment invalidates its information. Delayed messages cannot reactivate obsolete responsibility; duplicate declarations should be idempotent. Existing planner-owned revisions should be reused where adequate. Recorder observes these states rather than becoming their authority.

Task and authority changes cause auditable planning updates. Prediction thresholds require validation. A stale-anchor ablation can test the interruption mechanism, but stale responsibility is not permissible normal behavior. These are method and implementation requirements, not claims of completed production integration. General negotiation, proactive clarification, and new motion-planning algorithms remain outside the current contribution.

\section{Encounter Endpoint and Experimental Plan}
\subsection{Independent Sustained-Proximity Criterion}
An independent evaluator uses target truth $x^\star$. Radius $\rho_E$ and dwell $\tau_E$ define effective encounter time
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 T_E=\inf\{t\geq\tau_E:\;&\exists r,\ \|p_r(u)-x^\star\|_2\leq\rho_E\\
 &\text{for every }u\in[t-\tau_E,t]\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:event}
\end{equation}
Time is measured from mission start in simulation time. Each vehicle has an independent continuous timer; leaving resets it. Visits and vehicles cannot pool time. Autonomous, manual, and NAVIGATE vehicles are evaluated identically. No heading, field-of-view, or probability condition is added.

This endpoint is sustained proximity, not visual detection, confirmation, or rescue. Truth and target distance remain evaluator-only; an event result may be recorded and used for termination without entering the posterior. Sampled timing, invalid-pose handling, and resets must be specified before evaluation so missing data cannot establish dwell.

The intended observations at common budget $B$ are $Y=\min(T_E,B)$ and $\delta=\mathbf1[T_E\leq B]$. Radius, dwell, and formal budget remain open. The encounter relationship to existing confirmation-based FOUND/$\Gamma$ and same-tick deadline arbitration also remains open; implementation must reconcile these before formal trials. Budget processing delay must not extend the analysis horizon. Administrative STOP, invalid timing, and crashes require separate disposition rather than automatic ordinary budget censoring. Random POSITIVE is not encounter success.

\subsection{Conditions and Study Design}
The primary comparison is B2 versus B3, sharing the declaration interface, sensing model, clustering, assignment, controller, and budget. B0 all-autonomous and B1 human-control without a responsibility anchor are supporting references. Offline B4 uses actual future human motion as a perfect-information reference for the same mechanism, not a deployable predictor or guaranteed performance upper bound.

Paired replay holds human trajectory and declaration/cancellation events fixed within scenarios. Map, target placement, initial states, and sensing configuration are matched. Equal seeds alone do not imply identical observations when autonomous trajectories diverge; the coupling policy must be recorded. Replay isolates the mechanism under fixed human behavior. A later counterbalanced live study assesses interaction effects and cannot assume identical trajectories across conditions. Participant collection follows applicable institutional reporting and approval requirements.

Predictor selection and parameter validation use held-out development data, not final mission improvement. Formal sample size, participant/scenario structure, and the analysis protocol remain to be finalized before evaluation.

\subsection{Primary and Supporting Outcomes}
The primary estimand is
\begin{align}
 \mathrm{RMST}_c(B)&=\int_0^B P(T_E^c>t)\,dt,\\
 \Delta_E&=\mathrm{RMST}_{B3}(B)-\mathrm{RMST}_{B2}(B).
\end{align}
Negative $\Delta_E$ supports faster encounters under this protocol. Budget-limited failures are retained. Paired scenario bootstrap intervals are a candidate for replay; final resampling units and participant analysis must be predeclared.

Supporting evidence includes redistribution lead time, prediction error, fallback frequency, actual sensing overlap, encounter success by budget, travel, and planning cost. Planned responsibility overlap is not actual sensing overlap. An overlap definition must be validated against logging; no unverified hazard metric is frozen here. Optional workload and declaration-burden measures address interaction cost. Faster encounter alone does not establish reduced redundancy or improved operator experience.

\section{Implementation Status and Limitations}
The implementation target is an extended VRX/Gazebo/ROS~2 Humble system with ten WAM-Vs. Sensor-event inference, clustering, path-cost assignment, regional coverage, authority handling, and recording provide shared infrastructure. Full production acceptance of explicit SEARCH gating, interruption handling, prediction fallback, and the encounter evaluator remains pending. An engineering smoke test is not evidence of comparative mission benefit.

No formal B2/B3 outcome is reported for~\eqref{eq:event}. Required evidence includes predictor validation, responsibility/interruption execution, encounter timing/censoring, paired outcomes, and mechanism measurements. Completed RF validation, calibrated perception, gaze validation, or participant studies are not claimed.

The point anchor is a proxy, not a commitment to cover its entire induced region. Inaccurate prediction may harm allocation; frequent fallback may make B3 resemble B2 and must be reported. Tracking quality can confound comparisons. Assumed sensing and proximity-based success support a bounded simulation claim, not real-camera detection or field deployment.

\section{Conclusion}
This draft focuses on the value of predicting explicitly declared human search responsibility. B2 and B3 share inference and planning, differing in valid responsibility anchors. Interruption revokes obsolete responsibility. An independent sustained-proximity endpoint provides a simple simulation comparison. Benefits remain hypotheses pending engineering acceptance and formal experiments.
