- `METADATA_VERIFIED`: bibliographic metadata/abstract was verified, but the full paper was not fully reviewed in the current literature pass.

## Strongest novelty-pressure set

The papers that should receive explicit discussion rather than a generic grouped citation are:
- Xie et al. 2011: predicted next human search location + inhibition/complementary search.
- Heintzman et al. 2021: anticipated human search trajectories + robot search trajectory optimization.
- Krzysiak & Butail 2022/2025: a human-teleoperated reference robot within a search team + autonomous adaptation from human movement/knowledge.
- Ishii et al. 2023: predicted future human search regions + complementary collaborative search.
- Talebpour & Martinoli 2018/2019: human trajectory prediction/uncertainty + MRTA/replanning.
- Cortés et al. 2004 and Fu et al. 2009: classical spatial partition/responsibility/overlap foundations.
- Bourgault 2003 and Hollinger 2009/2015: classical Bayesian negative evidence, capture probability, and probabilistic multi-robot search.

## Claims that should NOT be used as novelty claims

Do not claim novelty in: Bayesian missed-detection updating; residual nondetection probability; Voronoi/CVT/Lloyd coverage; dynamic repartition; fixed/static resources influencing mobile redistribution; human future-motion prediction; future human search-region prediction; overlap reduction; online/event-driven replanning; human trajectory replay; prediction-error injection; MRTA/Hungarian; A*; directional probabilistic sensing in the abstract; or a human controlling one robot in a fleet.

## Current safest novelty kernel

**Predictive fleet-level responsibility reconfiguration under dynamic human takeover**:
a fixed-horizon forecast of the human-controlled member of the same probabilistic-search fleet is interpreted as **prospective search responsibility**, represented as an explicit planning generator/anchor; the full searchable domain is repartitioned jointly with the remaining autonomous responsibility generators; and this planning information is kept separate from target evidence.

The second candidate contribution is an **empirically validated perception-to-search probabilistic interface**. Its novelty does not lie in Bayesian filtering, but in calibrating a real directional detector, temporal observation dependence, and mission-level nondetection calibration end-to-end.
